This policy describes what the EvenUS app actually does with your information. Every claim below was written against the app's code and its database rules, not from a template.

The short version

EvenUS is an app for two people who live together. It compares how money, time and household work are shared between you, and suggests one thing to try each week.

Inside the app, your household's data is visible only to the two of you. It is stored with Google Firebase, which encrypts it in transit and at rest. It is not end-to-end encrypted, so it is technically readable on our servers; at EvenUS, access is limited to the few people who need it to answer a support request you made or to meet a legal obligation, and we do not browse it.
We never tell either of you what the other didn't do. There is no notification for that, and no setting to enable one. You can choose to hear what your partner added (a daily summary, each entry, or nothing), and each of you decides that for yourself.
Your weekly check-in answers are private from your partner, permanently and by design. The database rules enforce them as private; they are not merely hidden in the app.
We do not sell your data, and we do not show ads.
There is no analytics SDK in this app. No third-party tracker, no advertising identifier, no automatic screen recording.
Your calendar is off unless you switch it on, and even then only busy hours leave your phone, never what your events are. See Calendar busy time below.
Receipt photos never leave your phone. They are read by your phone's own text recognition and kept only on that phone.

Receipt scanning

If you scan a receipt, the photo is read on your phone, by your phone's own text recognition (Apple's Vision on iPhone, Google's ML Kit on Android). The amount, shop name and date it finds are filled into the expense form for you to check; only what you then save as the expense (the amount, the name you keep, the category and the day) is stored, exactly like an expense you typed.

The photo and its text never leave your phone. They are not uploaded to us, not stored in our database or file storage, and not sent to any other service. Our database rules and storage rules refuse them, so this is enforced on the server as well as in the app.
The photo is kept in EvenUS's own folder on your phone so you can look at it again, and the expense shows “Receipt saved on this phone”. Your partner never sees it: their phone has no copy.
Deleting the expense deletes the photo. Deleting your account deletes every receipt photo on that phone.
On Android, Google Play services may download the text-recognition model the first time you scan, and, as with any app using Google's ML Kit, may send Google basic diagnostic and usage information about the recognition itself (for example, device type and how long it took) under Google's terms. Your photos and the text read from them are never sent. App data is excluded from Android backups. On iPhone, if you have iCloud or computer backups switched on, the photo is included in your own phone backup like any app's files.

The light or dark appearance you choose is also kept only on your phone.

Importing and Apple Pay

Bank statements (CSV, OFX, QFX) and Splitwise exports are read on your phone. The file is never uploaded to us or anyone else; the app's copy is deleted as soon as it has been read. Only the rows you tick become expenses, stored exactly like expenses you typed (amount, name, category, day, who paid, how it is split). To spot things you have already added, the app looks at the expenses already in your household for the same dates (your statement is not sent anywhere to do this).
Apple Pay capture (iPhone, optional). If you set up the Shortcuts automation, the merchant name and amount of a Wallet payment are kept only on your phone (in the app's shared storage on the device) and the app asks you, with one notification on your phone, whether to add it. Nothing is sent to us until you choose Add and save the expense; Personal discards it. Anything you ignore is deleted after 14 days, and signing out deletes them all. We never see your card, your other payments, or anything else from Wallet.
Tax-year summary. When you ask for one, our server adds up your household's shared expenses for that year and sends the totals back to your phone, where the PDF and CSV are made. Nothing is kept on the server, and your partner's hidden personal spending is shown without its detail.
Joint account, money agreement and date-night ideas are stored with your household like other shared records, and both of you can see them. Each of you records only your own top-ups into a joint account, and each of you agrees to a money agreement only for yourself.

Calendar busy time (optional, off by default)

If you switch on Us → Household → Calendar busy time and choose which of the calendars already on your phone count (for example iCloud, Google, Outlook or Exchange accounts you have added to the phone):

Your phone reads, from those calendars only, when each event starts and ends and whether it is all-day, for the current week and the next seven days. It does this with your phone's own calendar permission: there is no sign-in to Google, Microsoft or Apple and no access token, and nothing goes through any other service.
The phone's calendar system hands the app each event; EvenUS keeps only the start, the end and the all-day flag and discards everything else on the phone immediately: titles, locations, attendees and notes are never stored, logged or uploaded. All-day entries are not counted.
From those times your phone works out how many hours of each day were busy, and how many of those hours fell outside usual working hours (weekdays 8:00–18:00), and uploads only those totals: seven numbers per week, and the same seven split by working hours (households/{household}/availability/{you}_{week}). Our database rules refuse any other field, so nothing else can be added to that record.
Your partner can see those busy-hour totals (the Sunday talk may say “a busy week coming up for you”), never your events. The fairness numbers count busy hours outside usual working hours, and busy hours within them beyond your usual paid work, as less free time that week (capped), and the weekly suggestion avoids your busy days.
Which calendars you chose is kept only on your phone. Disconnect switches it off and deletes every week of busy hours you uploaded; signing out clears the choice on that phone; deleting your account or leaving the household deletes your busy-hour records. Your phone's own calendar permission can be withdrawn at any time in its Settings.

Childcare, parental leave, home maintenance, hosting and pets

These are stored with your household like other shared records, and both of you can see them: childcare providers (name, kind, which children, cost and days; its cost becomes a regular bill), the occasions you plan (date, number of guests, menu, tasks, budget), home maintenance settings and tasks, and pets (name, kind, optional birthday). Parental leave is entered by each of you for yourself only: the dates, how much you are still working, and how much of your pay continues (shown on the timeline only; it never affects a score). Your partner can see it but cannot change it, and it is deleted with your account. An occasion's spending total is added up by our server from the expenses tagged with it, leaving out anything that is just one person's own.

Your watch (optional)

If you install EvenUS on an Apple Watch or a Wear OS watch, your watch talks only to your own phone, never to us. Your phone sends it your chores for today and the “to even up” line (with the amount hidden unless you have switched on Show amounts on widget), and never a score or anything about expenses. What you tell EvenUS on the watch, and a chore you mark done there, go back to your phone, where you check and save them like anything you type. The words travel between your own devices through Apple's WatchConnectivity or Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer (which may relay them through Google's servers, encrypted, when the watch and phone are not near each other); we receive nothing until you save it on your phone. Signing out clears the watch.

What your partner can and cannot see

This deserves its own section, because it is the question most people actually have.

Your partner CAN see

Every shared expense and bill, every chore and completion, your name, your working hours, your share of what you earn together (as a percentage, never your income itself, though with their own income they can work yours out from it), your financial obligations, your parental leave dates, your busy-hour totals if you switched on Calendar busy time, the household's scores and history, and every change either of you makes to a setting that affects the score.

Your partner CANNOT see

Your receipt photos, files you import, Apple Pay payments waiting on your phone, anything about your calendar events (titles, places, people, notes: these never leave your phone), your weekly check-in answers, your individual mental-load answers before you have both answered, your password, or your notification settings. If you turn on “Hide my personal spending detail” they see that a personal expense happened and its amount, but not its title, notes or category.

What we will never send

A notification telling one partner what the other didn't do. That notification does not exist in the app and there is no setting to turn it on.

What you can choose to receive

A summary of what your partner added that day (expenses, chores ticked off, reminders), each entry as it happens, or nothing. It is set per person under Preferences, defaults to a daily summary, and a personal expense your partner has hidden shows only as “a personal expense”. Reminders are sent to whoever they are for and never say who set them.

When someone leaves or deletes their account

When a member leaves a household (Us → Household → Your household → Leave household) or deletes their account while they are in one:

The person who stays is told, once: a notification, a card on This week and Us until they dismiss it, and an email to their address only if it is verified, in their language. It says “[Name] has left your EvenUS household” or “[Name] deleted their EvenUS account” (never a reason), that everything recorded stays with them, and one line on what happens to the subscription (see Payments).
The name of the person who left is kept only on that notice, which only the person who stays can read; everywhere else in the household it is replaced by “Former member”. Without a name on record it says “Your partner”.
After a leave (not a deletion), the notice keeps the leaver's verified email address only if the person who stays had invited exactly that address before (an invite they sent from EvenUS), so “Invite them back” can fill in an address they already had. Otherwise no address is kept and “Invite them back” opens an ordinary invite: we never hand anyone a sign-in address they did not already have. A kept address is never shown to anyone else, is not included in the stayer's data export, and is removed from every notice that holds it if that person later deletes their account.
A notice is deleted when the person it belongs to leaves that household too.
The person who leaves gets one short confirmation email to their own verified address, in their language: that they left, or that their account was deleted. The latter also says that a store subscription is not cancelled by deleting the account, with the App Store and Google Play links to cancel it. It is written before their data is deleted.
These are account emails: they are sent whatever the notification settings are, with fixed wording written on our server. Our record of each one (the address and the message) is deleted automatically after 30 days.
The person who left never sees anything recorded after they left.

When someone new joins: a fresh start, and your “Previous household”

If someone new joins after a partner has left (anyone other than the person who left coming back):

The two of you start completely fresh, in a new household: no money, chores, history, reports, reminders, goals, budgets, bills, subscriptions, joint account, money agreement, Sunday talks, survey answers, children, pets, events, helpers, shopping list, activity or imports from before. The new partner can never read anything from before: the database rules refuse it, and so does the app.
What comes with the person who stayed is only their own settings: their name, colour, income and how often they are paid, hours, commute, sleep and own time, tags, payment details and privacy choice, plus the household's name, currency and time zone. Children are not carried over (they can be added again).
The earlier household becomes the stayer's “Previous household” (Us → Previous household): private to them alone, read-only (nobody can add, change or remove anything in it), and kept until they delete it (any time, from that screen) or delete their account. Nobody else can read it: not the new partner, not the person who left. It is included in that person's data export.
Both sides are told before anyone joins: the joiner's screen says they'll start fresh together and that earlier records stay private to the person who invited them; the invite screen says the same to the person who stayed.
If the same person who left comes back instead, nothing starts again: everything stays as it was.

Reminders

You can set a reminder on a shared chore, and either partner can set one on any chore. The notification goes only to the person who owns that chore, and it never says who set it. The owner can switch any reminder off, immediately, and we do not tell the person who set it. Reminders are scheduled on your own device and are not sent through our servers.

Household reminders and events (birthdays, events, to-dos, document expiry dates and subscription renewals) are shared by both of you (every one is on both of your lists) and delivered by our servers only to whoever they are for, and they never say who set them either. A document reminder holds the kind of document, whose it is and its expiry date, never its number. Add to calendar and Export build a calendar file on your phone and hand it to your share sheet; nothing is uploaded. The file includes each reminder's title, date and note, so it is worth a look before you send it anywhere.

Tips during your free week

During your 7-day free trial only, EvenUS may show you short tips about its features as notifications: at most one a day, around 19:00, never on a Sunday. They are scheduled on your phone, not sent from our servers, and they stop when the trial ends or as soon as either of you subscribes. They are off unless you choose them: when EvenUS first asks about notifications you pick either “Turn on, with free-week tips” or “Turn on, reminders only” (or “Not now”), and nothing is pre-selected. You can switch them on or off any time in Us → Preferences, or turn off EvenUS notifications in your phone's settings. To pick a tip that is useful, the app keeps simple counts of which features you have used on your phone only; those counts are never sent to us or anyone else.

Who processes your data for us

We use a small number of service providers to run EvenUS. They process data only to provide their service to us.

Google Firebase (Authentication, Firestore database, Cloud Functions, Cloud Storage). What they do: hosts your account and household data and runs our server code. Firebase Authentication also sends the email that verifies your address. What they receive: everything described in this policy that is stored on our servers.
RevenueCat. What they do: processes subscription purchases and validates store receipts, and tells our server whether a subscription is active. What they receive: your EvenUS account ID (the random user ID from Firebase, not your name or email) so the purchase can be linked to your household, and the purchase and receipt details Apple or Google send (product, dates, renewal status). Never your card details.
Expo (exp.host push service, and Expo's app-update service). What they do: delivers push notifications to your phone, and delivers small app updates. What they receive: for notifications, the notification's text, which can include names, expense titles and amounts, and your push token; for updates, your app version and platform, and your IP address, as with any download.
Firebase Trigger Email extension, sending through our email provider. What they do: sends partner invitations, the help and feedback messages that reach our support inbox, and the account emails sent when someone leaves a household or deletes their account. What they receive: the recipient's email address and the message. Invitation and account emails use fixed wording written on our server; an account email's record is deleted after 30 days.
Apple App Store / Google Play. What they do: take payment for subscriptions, and handle cancellations and refunds. What they receive: your payment, under their own terms. We never see it.

Inviting your partner by email. An invitation stores your partner's email address with the invite so the app can connect them to your household when they sign up with it. The email itself is sent with fixed wording from our server; you cannot add your own text to it.

Calendar busy hours (optional, see above) go to Google Firebase only: seven numbers per week of busy hours, never event titles, places or attendees. Disconnect deletes them.

On Google Firebase (Firestore, Authentication, Cloud Storage, Cloud Functions) in the United States, region us-central1.

If you are in the UK or EU, this is a transfer outside your jurisdiction. It is covered by Google's Standard Contractual Clauses; see Google's Cloud Privacy Notice.

How long we keep it

While your account exists, we keep your household data so your history stays accurate. A fairness app that quietly changed last month's numbers would be worthless.
When you delete your account, your identity, your private check-ins, your recorded obligations, your parental leave and your calendar busy-hour totals are deleted immediately. Because deleting your account also takes you out of your household, the mental-load answers of everyone in it (yours and your partner's) are deleted too, and the questions start again for whoever remains (they were about the two of you).
Shared household history is not deleted while your partner still uses it. Your name is replaced with “Former member”. This is deliberate: erasing your side of a shared expense would silently rewrite every past split and score that the person you lived with still relies on.
If you were the last member, the entire household and everything in it is deleted.
Your “Previous household” (kept for you when someone new joined after a partner left) is deleted, whole, when you delete your account, or earlier, whenever you delete it from Us → Previous household. Nobody else can read it in the meantime.
If you leave a household (without deleting your account), your income, hours and payment details, your private check-ins, your obligations, your calendar busy hours and your parental leave plans are removed from it. The mental-load answers of everyone in it are deleted and the questions start again: they were about the two of you, and must never be read as being about whoever joins next. Shared history (expenses and chores) stays for the partner who remains, with you shown as a former member. Personal expense detail you hid stays hidden, after you have left too. On your phone, changes still waiting to be sent to that household are cleared, and so is any invite code held from before.
If you bought the subscription and it is still running, we keep one small record after you delete your account (we call it the billing owner record): your (now deleted) account ID, the household the purchase pays for, and when we may delete it and the RevenueCat record below, no name, email or payment details. The store keeps renewing a subscription until it is cancelled, and this is how a renewal (or a refund) still reaches the household it paid for, where it covers the person who stayed only until the period paid for when you left ends (see Payments). Once the subscription has run out, a daily clean-up deletes it automatically; you can also ask us to (support@evenus.app). If nothing is running when you delete your account, it is deleted with your account.
RevenueCat: deleting your account also deletes RevenueCat's customer record for your account ID (your purchase history and the receipt details Apple or Google sent), straight away if nothing is running, otherwise once the subscription has expired. While a subscription is still active or paid up to a future date, RevenueCat needs that record to tell us about the renewal or the end of it, so the household keeps exactly the access the period you paid for gives it; a daily job deletes the record after it runs out (it checks again if it was renewed meanwhile). Until then it is linked only to that random ID. If a request can't be made, we retry daily; email support@evenus.app and we will remove it within 30 days. Apple and Google keep their own purchase records under their own terms.
Backups and server logs may persist for up to 30 days.

Deleting your account

In the app: Us → Delete your account. It takes effect immediately and does not need your partner's permission. With no household yet, Delete account is at the bottom of the welcome, setup and “Check your email” screens. If you are the only member of your household, the household and everything in it are deleted too, along with any “Previous household” kept for you.

You can also request deletion by emailing support@evenus.app from your registered address. We will action it within 30 days.

Step-by-step instructions, without needing the app: evenus.app/delete-account

We send a short confirmation email to your verified address when your account is deleted (see When someone leaves or deletes their account).

Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription bought through Apple or Google. Cancel it in your store account too: on iPhone, Settings → your name → Subscriptions (apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions); on Android, Play Store → Payments & subscriptions (play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions). See Payments below.

Payments

EvenUS Pro is a subscription: $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year (US dollar reference prices; the App Store or Google Play shows the price for your country, including any tax). One subscription covers both partners in a household.

7-day free trial, inside the app, no card needed, once per person. It starts when your household is created and covers both of you; each person only ever gets one free week, so leaving and starting another household does not start a new one, and a change of partner never starts a free week (a new partner is covered by a plan you already pay for, or you choose one). It is run by the app, not the store, so nothing is charged when it ends and we collect no payment details for it. There is no store free trial or introductory offer, so if you subscribe during the free week, the remaining free days end and billing starts that day.
Purchases are made through Apple or Google, under their own terms, and processed for us by RevenueCat. We receive your subscription status (which plan, active or not, when it renews), never your card details.
Subscriptions renew automatically until you cancel. Cancel at least 24 hours before the current period ends in your store account: on iPhone, Settings → your name → Subscriptions (apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions); on Android, Play Store → Payments & subscriptions (play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions). Deleting the app or your account does not cancel it. Refunds are handled by Apple or Google.
When a partner leaves or deletes their account, the subscription stays with whoever bought it. If you stay and you pay, it continues, with a new partner too. If the person who left paid, you keep Pro until the period they had already paid for ends, or until someone new joins, whichever is first; then you (or your new partner) choose a plan. Their payment never covers a new partner; if they come back, it applies again. When someone new joins, the two of you move to a new household (see When someone new joins): a subscription the person who stayed bought moves with them, and nothing of the person who left is carried over. A refund ends it straight away, wherever its buyer is by then. A subscription covers people who stayed this way for one household only: if its buyer later leaves another household too, it gives that one no extra time. To route a refund, our billing record for the buyer lists the households their running purchase left (household IDs only). The free week is never given twice.
After the trial, or if a subscription lapses, the computed features lock, but you can always read everything already recorded, add expenses, tick chores off, export your data and delete your account. Nothing you recorded is deleted because a subscription ended.

Settling up between you. “Pay now” opens a payment app you or your partner already use (UPI apps, PayPal or Venmo, or a payment link one of you added) with the amount filled in. The payment happens entirely in that app, under its own terms. EvenUS never sees it, never confirms it, and records it only if you tell the app it went through. There is no Apple Pay option.

Cookies and this website

Everything above is about the app. This section is about evenus.app, the website you are reading.

The website uses Google Analytics to count visits and see which pages people read, and only with your permission. The first time you visit, a small banner at the bottom of the page asks whether you accept analytics cookies. Until you choose Accept, no analytics cookies are set. We use Google's Consent Mode, so before you accept, Google receives only a cookieless signal that a page was viewed. It carries what any web request carries, such as your IP address and browser type, but no cookie or identifier that could recognise you on a later visit.

If you choose Accept, Google Analytics sets cookies in your browser and Google receives your IP address, the pages you view and general information about your device. We use it to understand which writing is useful, and for nothing else. Advertising storage and ad personalisation stay switched off either way.

Your choice is remembered in your browser's local storage on this device, so the banner does not ask again. To change it, . Declining, or blocking scripts in your browser, changes nothing about how the site works.

Analytics is not connected to your EvenUS account, and it cannot be. The website has no way of knowing who you are, and nothing measured here reaches the app or your household's data. There are no advertising cookies and no ad network of any kind.

Beta and launch sign-up on this website

If you join the beta on evenus.app, we keep your email address, which phone you use (iPhone, Android or both), whether your partner will use it with you, and which page you signed up on. Nothing else: no name, and not your IP address. Your connection's IP address is turned into a one-way code, together with the date, so we can limit how many sign-ups come from one place in a day; the IP address itself is never stored, and the code is deleted automatically after a few days.

We use your address for two things only: to send your beta invite (Google Play testing for Android, TestFlight for iPhone, which means adding your address to that store's tester list) and one email when EvenUS launches. No newsletter, no marketing from anyone else, and your address is never sold or shared.

It is stored in Google Firebase, the same service that holds app data (see who processes your data), and nobody's browser or phone can read it. It is deleted automatically 12 months after you sign up (signing up again restarts that), or sooner if you ask: email support@evenus.app from that address and we will remove it. The legal basis is your consent, given by ticking the box on the form; you can withdraw it at any time.
