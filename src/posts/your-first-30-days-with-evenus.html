In your first 30 days with EvenUS you set up together, log about a week of real life, get your first weekly picture of how time, money and mental load are shared, and try one swap for two weeks. You should not expect a perfect score or a solved argument. A realistic first month ends with one concrete change tried and a clearer idea of what the disagreement is really about.

This is the honest version of the first month, including the parts that feel awkward.

What happens on day one?

Each of you creates an account, one of you creates the household, and the other joins with a six-character code. The store listing puts setup at about five minutes each.

You each enter your income and your paid work hours, because the app does not expect the same household load from someone working sixty hours as from someone working twenty. Then you add chores, either your own or from the 29 starter chores, including planning ones like meal planning and booking appointments. Each chore gets an owner, a rough time and a frequency.

The single most important step on day one is the second partner joining. Every number compares two people's weeks, so one person on their own sees very little. Setting up EvenUS with your partner walks through each screen.

What should you do in week one?

Live a normal week, log it once at the end, and answer the mental-load questions. Nothing dramatic happens in week one, and that is fine.

Log with estimates, not a stopwatch

You can tick chores off as you go, but most couples use the weekly catch-up. It pre-ticks the recurring chores against their usual owner, you untick what did not happen, and you are done in about thirty seconds. Estimates are the point. Nobody times the twenty seconds it takes to notice the milk is low.

Answer the mental-load questions separately

The app asks each of you who mainly handles areas like meal planning, the social calendar, money admin and, if you have children, school admin. You answer separately, and neither of you sees the other's answers until you have both finished. This is where the planning work starts to count. What the mental load is explains why it is weighted rather than counted as extra chores.

Take Maya and Jordan. In week one Jordan logs cooking four nights and the bins. Maya logs two nights of cooking, the school forms, the dentist booking and the weekly food order. By count they look close. By weighted hours they are not, because three of Maya's tasks involve remembering, deciding and chasing.

What does your first weekly report show?

A first reading, often with a note that there is not much data yet. Treat it as a starting point, not a result.

After the week closes, both of you get a short summary. The home screen shows which way things are heading, where the load sits in hours, and possibly a first suggestion. In week one there is no direction yet, so the first report is mostly a baseline.

Expect one surprise. For many couples the planning weight makes the picture look less even than either partner assumed. That is not the app taking a side. It is the invisible part of the work showing up for the first time. How to read your weekly fairness report explains each section and which parts to react to.

What happens in weeks two and three?

You get a suggestion: one specific swap, with the reason it would help now, offered as a two-week trial.

For Maya and Jordan, the suggestion might be that Jordan takes over the weekly food order, because his Sunday is lighter and the order is a planning task Maya is currently holding every week. Not "help more". One task, one person, one reason.

Either of you can say "Not this one", and no note goes to your partner about it. If you both say yes, the swap runs for two weeks. Either of you can stop it early with no explanation. The idea is to lower the stakes: two weeks is an experiment, not a promise.

During this stretch, keep doing the weekly catch-up and the private check-in. The check-in is two questions about how fair the week felt and how it was overall. Only you can read your answers. If only one of you answers, the result still appears, marked as one-sided.

What should you expect by day 30?

One swap finished, a decision to keep it or hand it back, and three or four weeks of trend, which is enough to see a direction.

When the swap ends, the app offers both answers with equal weight: keep it, or swap back. Neither is the right one. Then a new idea arrives the following week.

You may also see a "seen differently" note, where your answers about the same area do not match. Maya might say the social calendar is mostly hers while Jordan says it is shared. The app shows both without ruling. The useful question it leaves you with is "what part of this do you not see me doing?"

What will not happen in the first month?

The app will not settle who does more, and it will not fix a relationship that is already struggling. It helps you find out what the argument is really about, and do one thing about it this week. Ending "who did more?" fights covers why counting tasks never settles it.

  • No scoreboard. No streaks, badges or points, and your score is never shared publicly.
  • No chasing. There is no button to remind your partner, and no notification about what they did or did not do.
  • No disputes. Neither of you can edit or challenge the other's entries.
  • No instant change. One swap in a month is a good month.

If the picture stays poor for about two months with no movement, the app stops suggesting swaps, says so once, points you toward a counsellor, and goes quiet. That is deliberate. If conversations about chores are standing in for something bigger, a qualified couples counsellor is the right next step, not an app.

How does the trial fit into the first month?

There is a 7-day free trial with no card needed, then $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year, and one subscription covers both of you.

Be aware that the core loop is weekly, so the trial shows you one full cycle. If your partner takes a few days to join, you will see less of it. Inviting them on day one matters more than anything else you do. If you stop subscribing, everything you have recorded stays readable and exportable. EvenUS is currently in closed testing and not yet on the app stores.

Common questions

How much time does EvenUS take each week?

About thirty seconds each for the catch-up, plus a few seconds for the two check-in questions. Setup is the only longer step.

Can I start before my partner joins?

You can set up the household and add chores, but the app compares two people, so there is little to see until your partner joins.

What if we disagree with the first report?

That is common and useful. Find the line on the time or money screen you disagree with, and check whether something was missed in logging before deciding the picture is wrong.

Can we try the calculation before signing up?

Yes. The household fairness calculator runs the same hours sum in your browser, with no account needed.
