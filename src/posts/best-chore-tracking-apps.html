The best chore tracking app depends on what you need it to do. For keeping a home clean on a schedule, cleaning planners such as Tody and Sweepy are strong. For families with children, OurHome is built around chores and rewards. For flatmates, Flatastic handles rotas and shared costs. For money, Splitwise and Honeydue. For seeing whether the whole load is fair between partners, EvenUS.

Most people looking for a chore app are trying to solve one of five different problems, and the apps that are best at one are often poor at another. This guide sorts them by job, so you can pick the right type first and then the right app.

Which chore app is best for a cleaning routine?

If the problem is that the cleaning keeps piling up, a dedicated cleaning planner is the right tool, and Tody and Sweepy are both well-known examples.

Tody

Tody is a cleaning planner. You set up your rooms and the recurring jobs in each, and it shows which areas are due attention, so the cleaning gets spread across the week instead of piling up for Saturday. It suits anyone whose main complaint is "the house never feels on top of things" more than "I do more than you". It is the better choice over EvenUS if you mostly want a cleaning schedule.

Sweepy

Sweepy is also a cleaning schedule app organised around rooms and tasks, and it can be shared within a household so jobs can be divided. It suits couples and families who want a clear routine and like seeing the house broken down room by room.

The limit of both is the same, and it is not a flaw so much as a matter of scope. A cleaning app sees cleaning. It does not see paid work, the school forms, the dentist booking or the bills, and it does not ask who noticed the bathroom needed doing. If the cleaning is getting done but one of you still feels they are carrying the house, a cleaning planner will not show why.

Which chore app is best for families with kids?

OurHome is designed for families who want to give children regular jobs and reward them, which makes it a good fit for that specific task.

Family chore apps focus on assigning tasks to children, tracking whether they are done, and linking them to rewards. For a household with a seven-year-old and a ten-year-old who need structure, that is useful. It is a different problem from the one most couples argue about, though. The work of setting up the chart, deciding the rewards and remembering to check is itself part of the parents' load.

Which app is best for flatmates?

Flatastic is built for shared flats: a cleaning rota, a shared shopping list and a record of shared costs, in one place.

Flatmates generally want things equal and simple, and a rotating rota fits that well. It is a good fit for couples who live with others, or for a couple whose arrangement is genuinely flat-share-like: similar incomes, no children, an equal split. It is less suited to couples where incomes differ or where one partner carries most of the planning, because a rota assumes everyone's time and money are interchangeable.

Which apps are best for sharing money?

Splitwise for keeping separate finances with a running balance. Honeydue for couples who want a joint view of household money.

Splitwise is a bill-splitting app. You log shared expenses and it keeps a tally until you settle up, which works for couples, flatmates and friends alike. It is simpler than anything else on this list if money is your only concern. Honeydue is designed specifically for couples and aims to give both partners a shared picture of bills and spending.

Neither tracks chores, and that is fine. Money is often easier to discuss than housework precisely because it is already counted. EvenUS vs Honeydue vs Flatastic compares these approaches in more detail, and shared expense tools for unmarried couples covers money tracking on its own.

Why do chore apps often fail couples?

Usually because they track the doing and miss the deciding, or because they turn into a scoreboard.

An app that only records completed tasks makes the visible work visible, which helps. But someone still has to set the app up, add the tasks and notice what is missing, and that is often the same person who was already carrying the mental load. Points and streaks add a second problem. A streak punishes whoever had a hard week, and a points total between two adults who live together reads as a contest. How to choose tonight

  1. Name the problem in one sentence. "The house is always a mess" is a cleaning problem. "We fight about who pays" is a money problem. "I'm the only one who thinks about anything" is a fairness and mental load problem.
  2. Pick the app type that matches. Use the table above.
  3. Agree who sets it up. Ideally the person who usually does less of the planning.
  4. Review after two weeks. If only one of you is using it, the app is not the issue.

Common questions

Is there a free chore app for couples?

Several chore apps have free versions, and a shared notes or to-do app costs nothing. Check each app's current pricing, as plans change often.

Do chore apps work on both Android and iOS?

The well-known ones generally do, which matters if you and your partner use different phones. Confirm before you both set up accounts.

Can one app handle both chores and money?

Some do both in a basic way, Flatastic for flatmates for example. EvenUS covers both for couples but keeps them on separate axes rather than combining them.
