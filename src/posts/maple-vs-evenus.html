What a shared organiser does

Family organisers put the household's moving parts in one place both people can see. A shared calendar, lists that either partner can add to and tick off, meals, groceries, tasks that can be assigned to a person. Maple's own site describes its aim as helping families stay organised and connected, with connections to Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, Outlook and TeamSnap, which is this category.

The problem they solve is coordination. Two adults holding overlapping information in two heads, some of it out of date, and things falling between them. When it works, fewer things get missed and neither person is the sole source of truth.

That is a real problem and organisers are good at it. If your household's complaint is "we keep forgetting things", this is the category you want, and this article is not going to talk you out of it.

What EvenUS does instead

EvenUS does not organise the household. It measures how the load is shared and suggests one thing to change.

It starts from discretionary hours: what is left of each person's week once sleep, paid work, commuting and household work come out. It counts the planning and remembering each of you holds as hours too, because organising a birthday involves weeks of keeping it in mind, not only the afternoon of the party. It tracks money separately on its own axis, and never converts hours into currency.

Then, once a week, it says one specific thing to swap, and why now. Not a list. One.

The problem it solves is not coordination. It is the argument where one person feels they are carrying most of it and the other cannot see what they mean.

The difference that matters

A shared list tells you what needs doing. It does not tell you how the doing is distributed, and it cannot, because the hardest part of the load never reaches a list.

Consider a week where both partners tick off nine items each. Perfectly even, on the list. Now ask who added the items. Who noticed that the car needed a service before the light came on, who worked out that Tuesday was the only evening that worked, who has been carrying the fact that the passport expires in March.

That work does not appear anywhere on a shared list, because by the time something is on the list the thinking is already done. An organiser makes execution visible and leaves the management invisible, which is why a couple can run a tidy shared list for a year and still have the same argument. The mental load is about that gap.

Which should you use?

Use an organiser if your problem is memory. Use EvenUS if your problem is distribution.

A rough test. Ask both of you, separately, roughly what share of the household running you each do. If the two answers are close, you have a coordination problem and an organiser will help. If one says fifty and the other says eighty, no list is going to settle that, because you disagree about what is being counted rather than about what needs doing.

They also are not mutually exclusive, and nothing here needs you to drop one for the other. A shared calendar and a fairness measure do not overlap much. If you are still choosing an organiser, Cozi vs FamilyWall sets two of them side by side.

What EvenUS deliberately does not do

Worth stating, because some of it is what people come to an organiser for.

  • No task assignment or nudging. A reminder attaches to a task and goes only to whoever owns it, never labelled with who set it. Reminding your partner is not this app's job.
  • No shared to-do list. It is not where your household's tasks live.
  • No streaks, badges or points. A streak punishes whoever had a hard week.
  • No score shown for the other person. The output describes the arrangement, not either of you.

If those absences read as missing features, an organiser is genuinely the better fit for your household, and that is a real answer rather than a polite one.

The honest caveats

EvenUS needs both partners. It compares two people, so one person using it alone gets very little. An organiser degrades more gracefully when only one of you is keen.

It is in closed testing and not on the app stores yet. Maple, for its part, is on its way out: its website says it will shut down on 31 December 2026 and that all data will be deleted after that, and we could not find it on the App Store or Google Play on 29 October 2026. If you want an organiser you can install this afternoon, Cozi vs FamilyWall compares two that are available now.

It asks for some logging. Only the time you actually log counts, so someone has to record the chores, as you go or in a weekly catch-up that takes a minute or so each. The planning is estimated by area instead, from a few private questions, because nobody can log the seconds spent noticing. Some people want less upkeep than that, and an organiser asks for less.

Where each one stops

An organiser stops at the point where you both agree on the list and still disagree about the fairness of it. Nothing in a shared calendar can settle that, because the calendar only knows about the things that made it onto the calendar.

EvenUS stops at the point where you need to know what is for dinner on Thursday. It is not that kind of tool and it is not trying to become one.

Can we use Maple and EvenUS together?

Until Maple closes, yes. One keeps track of what needs doing, the other measures how the doing and the thinking are shared. They barely overlap, so running an organiser alongside EvenUS is a reasonable choice. Since Maple's site says it shuts down on 31 December 2026 and existing data will be deleted, a household starting now would do better to pick another organiser from the start.

Does EvenUS replace a shared calendar?

No. EvenUS has no shared calendar and no shared to-do list, and it is not where your household's tasks live. If you need to know what is for dinner on Thursday, an organiser is the tool for that.

Which is better for couples who keep arguing about who does more?

EvenUS, because that argument is usually about what is being counted, and a list only shows the doing. The mental load quiz is a quick way to see whether the planning side is part of it.

Can I download EvenUS today?

Not yet. It is in closed testing on iPhone and Android and is not on the App Store or Google Play. The fairness calculator runs the same hours sum in a browser in the meantime.
