A mental load checklist for couples lists the planning, remembering and tracking jobs behind each area of household life, so both partners can see who holds them. Go through each domain below together and, for every item, mark who notices it needs doing, who decides, and who does it. The pattern in the "notices" column is usually the real picture.

How do you use a mental load checklist?

Fill it in separately, then compare, then pick one area to move. It takes about forty minutes the first time.

  1. Each of you goes through the list alone. For each item you recognise, write an initial in three columns: Notices, Decides, Does. Write "both" only if it really is both.
  2. Cross out anything that does not apply. No children, no pets, no car: skip those sections. Add anything specific to your home.
  3. Compare. Where you disagree, do not argue about who is right. Each of you has seen a different part of the work. Mark the disagreement and move on.
  4. Count the Notices column by domain. A domain where one person notices almost everything is a candidate to hand over whole.

Use the checklist to agree a change, not to settle a score. It is a map, not a verdict.

Food and kitchen

  • Knowing what is in the fridge, freezer and cupboards
  • Planning the week's meals around who is home when
  • Remembering allergies, dislikes and diets, including guests'
  • Writing the shopping list and keeping it current
  • Noticing when staples are running low: oil, salt, coffee, nappies
  • Booking or changing the online delivery slot
  • Using up food before it goes off
  • Planning packed lunches and snacks
  • Deciding when the kitchen needs a deep clean or the oven needs doing
  • Replacing broken kitchen items and running-out cleaning products

Home and maintenance

  • Noticing repairs: dripping taps, blown bulbs, sticking doors
  • Finding, comparing and booking tradespeople
  • Being in, or arranging access, when they come
  • Boiler service, smoke alarm checks and gas safety dates
  • Bin and recycling days, including bank holiday changes
  • Seasonal jobs: gutters, garden, heating on and off
  • Knowing where the stopcock, fuse box and spare keys are
  • Keeping track of what needs replacing: mattress, towels, sofa
  • Decluttering and deciding what to give away
  • Dealing with the landlord or managing agent, if renting

Money and household admin

  • Knowing which bills go out when, and from which account
  • Spotting price rises and switching energy, broadband or insurance
  • Renewing home, car, travel and pet insurance
  • Tracking who has paid what for shared costs and settling up
  • Filing tax returns or keeping documents for them
  • Keeping important papers: passports, certificates, warranties
  • Cancelling unused subscriptions
  • Planning for big costs: holidays, Christmas, car replacement
  • Council tax, TV licence, parking permits and similar admin
  • Keeping passwords and account logins findable for both of you

Money is one area where an agreed method saves a lot of thinking. How to split bills when you earn different amounts sets out the main options.

Children and school

  • Term dates, inset days and half-term childcare
  • Reading school emails, apps and newsletters
  • Permission slips, payments, dress-up days and costume requests
  • Homework, reading logs and spelling tests
  • Knowing when uniform and shoes are too small
  • Booking and remembering clubs, lessons and kit
  • Arranging playdates and replying to party invitations
  • Buying presents for other children's parties
  • Childcare: finding it, booking it, paying it, covering gaps
  • Noticing worries about friendships, behaviour or learning
  • Parents' evenings and school reports

Health and wellbeing

  • Dentist, optician and GP check-ups for everyone
  • Vaccination schedules for children
  • Ordering and collecting repeat prescriptions
  • Keeping the first aid kit and medicines in date
  • Booking follow-ups after appointments
  • Noticing when someone seems unwell or low
  • Sorting care when a child is ill and someone has to stay home

Family, friends and social life

  • Birthdays and anniversaries on both sides of the family
  • Buying, wrapping and posting cards and gifts
  • Keeping in touch with parents, siblings and grandparents
  • Planning visits and who stays where
  • Replying to invitations and keeping the shared calendar
  • Organising date nights and babysitters
  • Thank-you messages after gifts or help
  • Checking on ageing parents and arranging support

Family care can become a domain of its own. If it has, caring for ageing parents covers how to share it.

Travel, holidays and occasions

  • Choosing and booking holidays
  • Checking passports, visas and travel insurance
  • Packing lists, especially for children
  • Arranging pet care, plant watering and post while away
  • Planning Christmas, Diwali, Eid or other festivals: food, gifts, visits
  • Booking restaurants, tickets and activities

Car, transport and pets

  • MOT, service, road tax and breakdown cover dates
  • Noticing tyre wear, warning lights and low screenwash
  • Parking permits and charging or fuel cards
  • Pet food, flea and worm treatments, vaccinations
  • Vet appointments and pet insurance claims
  • Kennels or sitters for trips

The relationship itself

  • Noticing when you have not had time together
  • Raising problems before they grow
  • Keeping track of what each of you said you would do
  • Remembering what matters to the other person this week

What should you do with the results?

Choose one domain where one partner does almost all the noticing, and move it to the other partner whole. Not a few items from it. The whole domain, including the remembering.

Common questions

How often should couples redo the mental load checklist?

Every three to six months, and after any big change such as a new baby, a new job or a house move. Those are the moments when the load shifts without anyone deciding it.

What if we disagree about who does something?

That is normal and useful. Mark it as a disagreement and ask each other what you each see happening. The gap often shows a hidden step, such as noticing, that only one of you does.

Should every item be split equally?

No. Splitting each item creates more coordination, not less. Owning whole domains works better, and the overall split should leave you both with similar free time.
