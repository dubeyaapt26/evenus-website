The best Honeydue alternatives for couples are Monarch and YNAB if you want a full shared budget with one subscription for both of you, Goodbudget if you like the envelope method and want a free plan, Splitwise if you only need to track who paid what, and Quicken Simplifi or Copilot Money if one of you runs the finances and the other wants a view in. Actual Budget suits couples who want open source and privacy. EvenUS is a different kind of pick: it splits shared costs by income and measures time as well as money, but it is not a bank-syncing budgeting app.

We should say up front that EvenUS is our own app. Every other app here is judged on what its official site says it does, as we found it in October 2026. Prices and features move around, so check each app's current page before you pay for anything.

Why couples move on from Honeydue

Honeydue describes itself as a simple way for couples to manage money together, with balances, bills, a budget, splitting and in-app chat on iPhone and Android. You choose what you want to share.

When people go looking for something else, it's usually because Honeydue is either too much or not enough. Some want a deeper budget. Some want less: a running tally of shared costs and nothing more. And some, like Alex and Sam, find the money side is mostly fine and the real friction is about time, chores and the planning one of them does without anyone seeing it. Which of those sounds like you decides which app below is worth a look.

1. Monarch: best all-round couples budget

Best for: couples who keep some money separate and some joint, and want all of it in one view.

Monarch calls itself a home base for money clarity. Its couples page talks about pulling separate and joint accounts into one place, with a shared budget and shared goals you can both put money towards even if that money sits in separate accounts. You can also tag your partner to look at a particular transaction. Each of you gets your own login, and Monarch says adding another member to the household costs nothing under the same subscription. It runs on iOS, Android and the web.

If what you liked about Honeydue was both of you seeing the same numbers, Monarch is the nearest thing to a fuller version of it. The catch is price. The pricing page lists no free plan: you get your first week free and a money-back guarantee, then it's $99.99 a year or $14.99 a month on the US site.

2. YNAB: best for a hands-on budgeting method

Best for: couples who want to plan every pound or dollar together, rather than just watch where it went.

YNAB (You Need A Budget) runs on iOS, Android, the web and watchOS, and balances and transactions sync in real time across devices. Up to six people can share one subscription, so the two of you don't pay twice.

It suits couples who are happy to sit down together and decide where the money goes before it's spent. There's no free plan on the pricing page. Sign up directly and you get a 34-day free trial with no card needed, then it's US$109 a year or US$14.99 a month, plus tax where it applies.

3. Goodbudget: best free envelope budget

Best for: couples who like the envelope method and want to start without paying.

Goodbudget is built around envelope budgeting and works on the web, Android and iPhone. Budgets sync and can be shared, so you both spend from the same envelopes. The Free Forever plan gives you 10 regular envelopes, 10 more envelopes, 1 account, 2 devices and 1 year of history. Premium is $10 a month or $80 a year and adds unlimited envelopes and accounts, 5 devices and 7 years of history.

For a simple shared spending plan it's hard to argue with free. Just know that automatic bank sync is a Premium feature and only works with US banks, so if you live anywhere else you'll be typing transactions in by hand.

4. Splitwise: best for simply splitting shared costs

Best for: couples who keep separate finances and only need to know where the shared costs stand.

Splitwise keeps track of shared expenses and balances with housemates, trips, groups, friends and family. It's free on iPhone, Android and the web. The paid Pro tier removes limits and ads and adds receipt scanning, itemisation, currency conversion, charts, search and default split settings, plus card transaction import (US only).

If splitting was the part of Honeydue you used most, Splitwise is the lighter option. It isn't a budgeting app, though, and it treats a couple much like any other group of people sharing costs. There's no single published Pro price either; the App Store says it varies by plan and country.

5. Quicken Simplifi: best for cash-flow planning

Best for: couples where one person runs the money and the other wants to see the plan.

Simplifi is a cloud-based personal finance app on web and mobile. It builds a spending plan for you automatically and has savings goals, reports, investment tracking and projected cash flow. It connects to more than 14,000 financial institutions. Through its spaces and sharing feature you can add one other person, such as a partner.

Pick it for the projections, which show what's likely to be left at the end of the month before you get there. Sharing stops at one other person, and the page doesn't mention a free trial. When we looked, prices were shown per month, billed annually, next to first-year offers that end on 13 November 2026, so check the current page before you pay.

6. Copilot Money: best for Apple households

Best for: couples who both use Apple devices and want spending, budgets, investments and net worth together.

Copilot Money is on iPhone, iPad, Mac and the web. It costs $95 billed yearly (about $7.92 a month) after a one-month free trial, and the homepage describes no free tier.

It makes sense if you're both fully on Apple. Android isn't mentioned, and nor is partner or shared access, which is why we'd rank Monarch or YNAB above it for couples unless one of you is happy to manage it alone.

7. Actual Budget: best open-source option

Best for: couples who want control of their data.

Actual Budget is an open-source envelope budgeting app with a focus on privacy. You can run its sync server yourself or pay for hosting through PikaPods. Bank syncing is built in, through GoCardless in the EU and UK and SimpleFIN in the US and Canada, and end-to-end encryption is there if you want it. No price is listed; the project takes donations through Open Collective.

Privacy comes at the cost of convenience here. Giving each partner a separate login means connecting an OpenID provider to your server, and setting the whole thing up is more work than downloading an app from a store.

Also worth knowing: EvenUS, for fairness rather than budgeting

Best for: couples whose money talks keep turning into who does more, or whose incomes differ enough that 50/50 doesn't feel fair.

EvenUS is our app, and it does a different job from Honeydue. It looks at how money, time and the planning nobody sees are shared between two people who live together. For money, you pick one of four ways to split shared costs: by income, evenly, ending up with the same, or splitting what is spare.

Then it counts the hours each of you has left in a week once paid work, travel, the house and the children are included, and suggests one change a week to try for two weeks. One subscription covers both of you. There's a 7-day free trial with no card, then $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year (USD reference prices, set per region by the app stores).

It's the right fit if the question in your home is less "where did the money go?" and more "is the way we share all of this actually fair?". You can try the idea now with the fair split calculator, or see where you each stand with the mental load quiz.

What it won't do is replace a budgeting app. EvenUS has simple shared monthly budgets for the categories you choose and can import a statement file you download from your bank, but it doesn't connect to your accounts or track investments or net worth. It's also still in closed testing, so you won't find it in the App Store or Google Play yet. For a head-to-head, see EvenUS vs Honeydue vs Flatastic.

Picking the right one for the two of you

Feature lists all start to look the same after a while. It's easier to start from what's actually bothering you, so look for the line below that sounds most like your home.

  • "We want one clear picture of all our money." Monarch, YNAB or Simplifi.
  • "We want a budget but don't want to pay yet." Goodbudget's free plan, or Actual Budget if you're happy to self-host.
  • "We keep money separate and just need to settle shared costs." Splitwise. Our guide to managing household finances without a joint account may help too.
  • "Our incomes are different and 50/50 feels wrong." Read how to split finances when incomes differ, try the calculator, and have a look at EvenUS.
  • "The money is fine. It's the time and the planning." Start with what the mental load is, because a budgeting app won't show you that side.

Before either of you signs up, check two practical things: that the app works on both your phones (Copilot Money doesn't mention Android), and whether one subscription covers you both.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to Honeydue?

Goodbudget has a Free Forever plan with a limited number of envelopes, accounts and devices. Splitwise is free for splitting shared costs, with a paid Pro tier on top. Actual Budget is open source and lists no price, though you'd need to self-host it or use a hosting service.

Is Zeta still a good Honeydue alternative?

No. Zeta shut down in 2025: members were told to close their accounts by 9 May 2025, and Acorns bought its assets in June 2025. The app and its website are gone, so it is no longer an option.

Which Honeydue alternative lets both partners log in on one subscription?

Monarch says adding household members costs nothing extra, and each partner has their own login. YNAB lets up to six people share one subscription. Simplifi lets you add one other person. EvenUS covers both partners with one subscription.

Can EvenUS replace Honeydue?

Only partly. EvenUS splits shared costs fairly, including by income, and brings time and the mental load into the picture. It doesn't sync bank accounts or work as a full budgeting app, so if you rely on Honeydue for balances and bill tracking you may want a budgeting app alongside it.

What is the difference between a budgeting app and a bill-splitting app?

A budgeting app plans where money goes. A bill-splitting app records who paid for shared costs and how they're divided. Our guide to income-proportional bill splitting goes into the second in more detail.
