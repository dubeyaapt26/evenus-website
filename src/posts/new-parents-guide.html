New parents split things fairly by planning three things on purpose: the nights, the baby-care areas, and the money. Divide nights into fixed shifts so both of you get one long block of sleep. Give each person whole areas of baby care and housework, rather than a list of separate tasks. Re-run your bill split the month parental leave pay starts. Without these decisions, the arrangement forms on its own around whoever is at home, and it is much harder to change later.

This guide is the practical setup for the first year. For the emotional and physical side of the months around birth, read pregnancy and postpartum alongside it.

Why does the split go wrong after a baby?

Because the parent on leave becomes the default for everything, and that default outlasts the leave. In the early weeks, it makes sense that the person at home handles feeds, washing, appointments and the shopping. By the time they go back to work, they also know the feeding schedule, the nappy size, the health visitor's name and the vaccination dates. The knowledge has built up in one head.

Their partner usually wants to help. But they are now working from instructions, which makes the parent on leave the manager of the baby as well as its main carer. That is how a new baby turns into a long-term mental load imbalance. What the mental load is explains why this kind of work stays invisible.

How should new parents split night feeds?

Use fixed shifts instead of whoever wakes up first. Two people half-waking for every feed means neither of them gets real rest. One person fully responsible for a set block of hours means the other can sleep properly.

A common setup for Maya and Jordan, with a bottle-fed or expressed-milk baby:

  • Jordan: 9pm to 2am. Maya sleeps in another room with earplugs if she can.
  • Maya: 2am to 7am. Jordan sleeps undisturbed before work.

If the baby is breastfed, the parent who is not feeding can still take a share of the night. They can bring the baby to the feeding parent, change the nappy, settle the baby afterwards, and take the first early-morning wake-up so the feeding parent can sleep on. Swap shifts at weekends if one of you is consistently worse off.

Feeding decisions and postnatal health are questions for your midwife, health visitor or GP. If either of you feels persistently low, anxious or unable to cope, talk to one of them early. That applies to both parents.

How do you divide baby care and housework fairly?

Give each parent whole areas, including the planning, not a list of tasks to help with. "Can you do bath time?" keeps one person in charge. "Bath and bedtime are yours" moves the job, the timing and the problem-solving.

Here is one way Maya and Jordan divided things once Jordan's paternity leave ended and Maya was still on leave:

On paper, Maya's day looks lighter. It is not. A day alone with a newborn is full-time work with no breaks you can schedule. That is why the evening and household areas go to the parent who is at paid work. The aim is for both of you to end the week with a similar amount of time that is your own, even if that is very little for now. Eve Rodsky's Fair Play (2019) uses the same principle: whoever holds an area handles it from start to finish. Dividing housework without it becoming an argument covers the method in more detail.

Keep the parent at work properly involved

Give the working parent at least one baby area they handle alone, with no supervision. It feels slower at first. It is also the only way two parents end up equally capable, and it is what stops the "you just know how to do it" pattern before it starts.

How should new parents split money during parental leave?

Recalculate your bill split for the leave period, because an income split that was fair before the baby can become unaffordable for the parent on leave. Statutory and employer leave pay vary a lot by country and employer, so check your own figures.

Maya and Jordan have £2,000 a month of shared costs. Before the baby, Jordan took home £3,000 and Maya £2,000, and they split costs in proportion to income:

  • Total income: £3,000 + £2,000 = £5,000
  • Jordan: 3,000 ÷ 5,000 = 60%, so £1,200
  • Maya: 40%, so £800

On leave, Maya's pay drops to £800 a month. If nothing changes, her £800 share takes her entire income. Here are the options:

MethodJordan paysMaya paysLeft over each
Keep old split£1,200£800£1,800 / £0
Proportional to new incomes (3,000 ÷ 3,800)£1,579£421£1,421 / £379
Residual (both keep the same)£2,000 and transfers £100£0£900 / £900

The residual method works like this: total income is £3,800, and after £2,000 of costs there is £1,800 left, so each keeps £900. Jordan covers all the costs and moves £100 to Maya. A middle option is "equal remainder", where each of you first keeps a protected personal allowance and the rest is split. Any of these can be fair. What matters is that you choose one together, because otherwise the parent on leave ends up asking for money, which neither of you wants. For bigger questions, such as pensions during leave, childcare costs or a longer career break, a qualified financial adviser can look at your real situation.

What should you agree before the baby arrives?

Agree the defaults while you both still have time to think. A one-hour conversation in the third trimester is worth more than a dozen 3am negotiations. Cover these:

  1. Who takes which night shift, and when you will swap.
  2. Which baby and household areas each of you owns, once both of you are home and again once one returns to work.
  3. Which split method you will use during leave, and the date you will switch back.
  4. Your visitor rules, and who tells the family.
  5. A date six weeks after the birth to review all of the above.

The review is the step most couples skip, and the one that matters most. Babies change every few weeks, so the arrangement should change too. Common questions

Is it fair for the parent on leave to do all the housework?

Not usually. Caring for a newborn alone all day is already full-time work. A fair split often gives most evening and weekend housework to the parent who is at paid work.

When should we redo the split after the baby is born?

At six weeks, when one parent goes back to work, and when nursery or childcare starts. Each of these changes who has spare time.

What if one of us earns nothing during leave?

Use the residual or equal remainder approach so the parent on leave still has personal money that is theirs, without having to ask for it.

How do we stop arguing about who is more tired?

Stop comparing tiredness and compare hours instead. Fixed night shifts and owned areas make the question factual, which makes it much easier to settle.
