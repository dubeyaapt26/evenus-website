To manage debt as a couple, first list every debt both of you have, with balance, interest rate and monthly payment. Then decide which debts are shared and which stay personal, agree how much each of you puts towards repayment, and pick one payoff order, usually highest interest first. Debts in one person's name generally stay theirs, but the plan works best when both of you can see it.

Debt is one of the hardest money subjects for couples because it often arrives with history: a loan from before you met, a card that grew during a bad year, a student loan one of you barely thinks about. This piece is about the practical plan. It is general information, not financial advice, and the professional help section below matters if the numbers feel unmanageable.

Is my partner's debt my debt?

Usually not, if it is in their sole name, but joint debts are different. In many countries, including the UK, a debt in one person's name is legally theirs, and you are not liable for it simply because you live together or marry. Rules vary, though, so check yours.

Joint debts work differently. With a joint loan, joint overdraft or joint mortgage, each of you can usually be pursued for the whole amount, not half. Acting as a guarantor makes you responsible if the borrower cannot pay. And in the UK, holding a joint account or joint credit can link your credit files, so one person's missed payments may show up when the other applies for credit.

Legal liability is only part of it. Even when a debt is technically one person's, its monthly payment shapes what the household can afford. That is why debts belong in the "share fully" group described in financial transparency in a relationship.

Step 1: Put every debt on one page

Write down each debt with five facts: whose it is, the balance, the interest rate, the minimum monthly payment, and whether it is joint. Nothing is decided at this stage. It is just the list.

Step 2: Decide whose debt is part of the household plan

There are three reasonable approaches, and the right one depends on how merged your finances are.

  • Personal debts stay personal. Each repays their own from personal money. Clean and simple, but if one person carries far more debt, they may have nothing left for themselves for years.
  • All debt is household debt. Every repayment comes from the shared pot. This suits fully merged finances and long-term commitment.
  • Hybrid. Joint debts and debts taken on for the household come from the shared pot. Debts that pre-date the relationship, or were for personal spending, stay personal, perhaps with some help from the other partner by agreement.

Maya and Jordan chose the hybrid. The car loan was joint and paid from the shared pot. The two cards were personal, but they agreed the shared pot would also contribute £250 a month of overpayments towards whichever card came first in their plan, because both cards were costing the household money in interest.

Step 3: Choose a payoff order

Pay the minimum on everything, then put every spare pound at one debt at a time. There are two well-known ways to choose which one.

Avalanche: highest interest first

You target the most expensive debt first. This costs the least in interest overall.

Snowball: smallest balance first

You target the smallest balance first, for a quick win. It can cost more in interest, but some people stick with it better.

For Maya and Jordan, both methods start in the same place: the store card has the highest rate and the smallest balance. After that, avalanche and snowball both point to the credit card, because it has a higher rate than the car loan and a smaller balance.

To see why the order matters, look at roughly what each debt costs in interest in the first month. Divide the APR by 12 and multiply by the balance:

  • Store card: £800 × 29% ÷ 12 ≈ £19
  • Credit card: £3,200 × 24% ÷ 12 = £64
  • Car loan: £6,000 × 7% ÷ 12 = £35

That is around £118 a month in interest, before anything comes off the balances. These are approximations, since lenders calculate interest in different ways, but they show where the money is leaking.

Paying £275 a month at the store card (the £25 minimum plus the £250 extra), the balance falls from £800 to about £544 after the first month, then about £282, then about £14, and it is cleared in the fourth month. From then on, the £275 rolls onto the credit card on top of its own £96 minimum, so £371 a month goes at Jordan's card.

Step 4: Make it fair, not just efficient

Check what each of you has left after shared costs and debt repayments, not just who pays which bill. A plan that clears debt fast but leaves one partner with nothing of their own tends not to last.

If incomes differ, contributions to shared costs and joint debts can be split in proportion to income or with a protected personal allowance first. How do you talk about debt without blame?

Talk about the debt as a problem the household is solving, not as a verdict on the person who took it on. Debt often comes from a period of low income, illness, a family crisis or simply not being taught about interest. How it arrived matters less than the plan for clearing it.

Agree, before the first conversation, that it is only for getting the list right. Decisions come later. If a debt has been hidden until now, that is a trust issue as well as a money one, and financial infidelity in relationships covers how couples handle it. When should you get professional help with debt?

If you are missing payments, borrowing to repay other debts, or the minimum payments take more than you can comfortably afford, get free advice before it gets worse. In the UK, StepChange and Citizens Advice offer free, confidential debt advice. In the US, non-profit credit counselling agencies that belong to the NFCC can help. Elsewhere, look for a free, non-profit debt advice service, and be wary of any company that charges large fees to "fix" debt. A regulated financial adviser can help with longer-term planning once the debt is under control, and a couples counsellor can help if debt keeps causing conflict.

Common questions

Should we combine our debts into one loan?

Consolidating into a joint loan can lower the rate, but it also turns personal debts into joint ones, making both of you liable. Get free debt advice before doing it.

Should we pay off debt or save first?

Many people keep a small emergency fund while paying down high-interest debt, so a surprise bill does not go back on a card. The right balance depends on your situation, so check with a free adviser.

Does marrying someone with bad credit affect my credit score?

In the UK, marriage alone does not merge credit files, but joint accounts or joint credit can link them. Rules differ in other countries.

Is it unfair to help pay my partner's debt from before we met?

It is a choice, not an obligation. Some couples help because the interest costs the household; others keep old debts personal. Agree it explicitly and write it down.
