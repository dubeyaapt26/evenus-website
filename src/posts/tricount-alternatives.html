The best Tricount alternatives for couples are Splitwise if you want more ways to split and a web version, Settle Up if you want a free app that also runs in a browser, Splid if you want something just as simple, and Honeydue or Monarch if you have outgrown splitting and want to see your money together. EvenUS is a different kind of pick: it splits shared costs by income as well as evenly and looks at time and chores too, but it is not a group expense app and it only works for two people.

Why couples look beyond Tricount

Tricount is a good app, and for many couples there is no reason to leave. It is free, with no ads and no limits, its help center says it runs on iOS and Android, and its US site lists couples splitting "the weekly shop, date nights, and more" as one of its uses. Its blog even suggests proportional splits for when one partner earns more. Tricount joined bunq, a European bank, in 2022, and its old Premium tier was discontinued with the new app.

The reasons people look elsewhere tend to be practical. Some want a web version on a laptop. Some want a split that is remembered for every expense rather than set each time. Some want to see joint and separate accounts in one place, which a splitting app was never meant to do. And some find the money is not really the problem: it is who does the laundry and who remembers the dentist. Which of those sounds like your home decides which app below is worth a look.

1. Splitwise: best for more ways to split

Best for: couples who log a lot of shared costs and want a set split remembered for them.

Splitwise is free on iPhone, Android and the web, and it lists couples sharing relationship costs among its uses. The free tier lets each person add up to 4 expenses a day. The paid tier, Splitwise Pro, removes that cap and adds things like a group default split: its own page gives the example of splitting everything with your partner 55/45. Pro can also import purchases from a connected credit or debit card, which its site says is currently available only in the US.

Its help center says a Duo plan can be used by two people, so one of you can add the other. Splitwise does not print a Pro price on its website; the App Store lists several Pro price points without saying which plan each one is, so check the price in the app. If the daily cap is the only thing that bothers you about free apps, Splitwise's free tier will feel tighter than Tricount. For a deeper look, see Splitwise vs Tricount and Splitwise alternatives for couples.

2. Settle Up: best free option with a web app

Best for: couples who want a Tricount-style app they can also open on a computer.

Settle Up runs on iOS, Android and as a web app, and it names couples alongside travelers and roommates as who it is for. Its App Store listing describes even splits, unequal weights and several people paying for one item, and a group link your partner can open in a browser without downloading anything. The app is free with ads. Premium removes ads and adds receipt photos and recurring expenses. Settle Up's own site does not print prices, so check the current Premium price in the app or on its App Store listing.

If you liked Tricount's simplicity and only miss a browser version, this is the closest swap.

3. Splid: best for keeping it simple

Best for: couples who want a shared tally and nothing else.

Splid says plainly that it is free. You share a group online with no sign-up, and its App Store listing mentions splitting bills unequally. Its App Store listing says Excel export is an in-app purchase; Splid's site does not price its extras. The App Store version is for iPhone, and Splid also has a Google Play listing.

It suits couples who travel or share a few costs and do not want accounts or settings. It does not remember an income-based split or do anything beyond the tally.

4. Honeydue: best for seeing money together

Best for: couples who want to see balances and bills in one place, not just who owes whom.

Honeydue is free on iPhone and Android, with optional monthly tips in the App Store. It connects to banks (its site says over 20,000 institutions across 5 countries), lets each partner choose how much to share, reminds you when bills are due, and lets you react to or ask about a transaction. Its Joint Banking program is still a waitlist on its site.

This is a step up from splitting, toward a couples money app. If the bank connection is what you want, Honeydue costs nothing to try. Our Honeydue alternatives page covers what to try if it is not enough.

5. Monarch: best for a full shared budget

Best for: couples who keep some money joint and some separate and want all of it in one view.

Monarch works on the web, iOS and Android. Its couples page talks about bringing separate and joint accounts together into one view, and says partners each get their own login and adding household members costs nothing extra under the same subscription. Note that its couples page also says both of you see all accounts and transactions. It costs $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year after a 7-day trial; there is no free plan.

This is more than a Tricount replacement: it is a budgeting app. If you want that, see Monarch vs YNAB or our Monarch alternatives.

Where EvenUS fits

Best for: couples whose incomes differ enough that 50/50 does not feel fair, or whose arguments are as much about time as money.

EvenUS is our app, and it is built for two people who live together, not for trips or groups. For shared costs you pick one of four ways to split: by income, evenly, ending up with the same, or splitting what is spare. If you are coming from Splitwise, it can import a group export; it has no Tricount import.

It also counts the hours each of you has left in a week after paid work, travel, the house and the children, and suggests one change a week to try for two weeks. One subscription covers both of you: a 7-day free trial with no card, then $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year (USD reference prices, set per region by the app stores).

Its limits matter here. EvenUS never connects to a bank, works only for two people, and is not built for trips with friends or a shared house. It is also still in closed testing, so you cannot download it from the App Store or Google Play yet. If you want to see how an income-based split would look for you, try the fair split calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tricount still free in the US?

Yes. On 3 November 2026 its US site said "Free. No ads. No limits.", and its US App Store listing showed no in-app purchases. Its help center says Premium was discontinued with the new app.

What is the closest free alternative to Tricount?

Settle Up and Splid are both free to use and work the same way: a shared group, expenses, and a balance. Settle Up shows ads on its free tier and has a web app; Splid is free with optional paid extras.

Which Tricount alternative lets us split by income?

Splitwise Pro lets you set a default percentage split for a group. EvenUS can split each shared cost by income, worked out from what each of you earns. Tricount's own blog suggests proportional splits too, entered per expense.

Can EvenUS replace Tricount?

For a couple's shared costs at home, largely yes. For trips with friends or a shared house, no, because EvenUS only works for two people. It is also not on the app stores yet.
