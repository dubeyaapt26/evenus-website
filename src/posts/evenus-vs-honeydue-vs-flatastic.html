When is Honeydue the better choice?

When your main need is managing money together: seeing shared spending and budgeting as a couple.

Honeydue is built around couples' finances. If your arguments are about spending, saving or not knowing where the money goes each month, a dedicated money app is the right category, and EvenUS is not trying to replace it.

EvenUS handles money differently and more narrowly. It does not connect to your bank and is not a budgeting tool. It asks whether shared costs are split the way you agreed (split evenly, split by income, “end up with the same”, or “split what's spare” after a personal floor) and whether each of you has personal money left afterwards. It also tracks settlements between you. Money stays on its own axis and is never converted into hours of housework.

Take Priya and Tom. Priya takes home £3,000 a month, Tom £2,000, and their shared costs are £2,000. Split 50/50, each pays £1,000, leaving Priya £2,000 and Tom £1,000. Split in proportion to income, Priya pays 60% (£1,200) and Tom 40% (£800), leaving £1,800 and £1,200. A budgeting app helps them see the spending. EvenUS helps them see whether the split itself feels fair. How to split bills when you earn different amounts covers the methods in full.

When is Flatastic the better choice?

When you need to coordinate chores and shopping between the people in one home, whether that is flatmates, a family or a couple.

Flatastic is a shared-household app for chores and shopping, with a tracker for shared expenses alongside, and its own site says it is for shared flats, families and couples. If your problem is organisation, such as whose turn it is to clean the bathroom or who is buying washing-up liquid, that is what this category exists for. With three or four flatmates especially, a shared rota tool is a better fit than EvenUS, which is designed around two partners.

The difference is in what gets counted. A chore app records tasks being done. It cannot easily record the planning that goes into them: noticing the bathroom needs doing, remembering the landlord inspection, deciding which cleaner to buy. Between flatmates, that matters less. Between partners, it is often the whole argument. What the mental load is explains why.

What does EvenUS do that the others don't?

It measures fairness across time, money and mental load together, and turns that into one specific suggestion a week.

It starts from time, not task counts

Everyone has 168 hours a week. EvenUS takes out sleep, paid work, commuting, household work and the planning each of you holds, counted as hours, and compares the hours each of you has left. That handles the case where one of you works long paid hours and does less at home, without treating either of you as doing too little. How the EvenUS fairness score works explains the model.

It suggests one swap, not a list

Each week it suggests one change: who, what, and why now. Either of you can say no, and if you both agree you try it for two weeks.

It shows where you see things differently

When your answers about who handles an area do not match, it shows both without taking a side.

It deliberately leaves things out

No streaks, badges, points or leaderboards. No way to nudge your partner. Reminders belong to a chore, reach only its owner, and never say who set them. No bank connection, and no public sharing of your score.

Where does EvenUS fall short?

It is not a budgeting app, not a rota for a shared flat, and not yet available to download.

  • It needs both partners. Every result compares two people, so one person alone sees very little.
  • It is built for two. A household of four flatmates is better served by a shared chore app.
  • It does not track spending in detail. If you want a full budget, use a money app alongside it or instead.
  • It is in closed testing. Honeydue and Flatastic are shipped products you can install today. EvenUS is not on the app stores yet.
  • It is paid after a trial. A 7-day free trial with no card, then $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year, with one subscription covering both of you.

Which app should you choose?

Ask what you actually argue about. A quick test:

  1. "We never know where the money goes." A couples money app such as Honeydue.
  2. "Nobody knows whose turn it is." A shared chore app such as Flatastic.
  3. "One of us feels they carry everything, and the other can't see it." That is a fairness question, which is what EvenUS is for.

They are not mutually exclusive. A couple could use a money app for the budget and EvenUS for how the money and work are shared. For two people who disagree about who does more, no amount of organisation settles it, because the disagreement is about what is being counted. Ending "who did more?" fights covers that. Common questions

Can flatmates use EvenUS?

The money side works the same way for housemates who split bills. The time and mental-load side is designed around two partners, so a larger flat is usually better served by a shared chore app.

Does EvenUS connect to my bank?

No. There is no bank connection. You add shared expenses and bills yourself.

Is there a free way to try the EvenUS approach?

Yes. The household fairness calculator runs the same hours sum in your browser, with no account and nothing stored.
