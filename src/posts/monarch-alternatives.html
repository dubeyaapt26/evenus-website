Why couples look beyond Monarch

Monarch is one of the most complete couples budgeting apps. It works on the web, iOS and Android, pulls joint and separate accounts into one view, gives each partner their own login, and says adding household members costs nothing extra under the same subscription. Its US App Store rating was 4.9 from 117K ratings when we checked.

The reasons to look elsewhere are usually price, method or privacy. Monarch has no free plan: after a 7-day trial it is $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year. Some couples want a stricter budgeting method rather than a dashboard. And Monarch's couples page says both partners have full visibility into all accounts and transactions, which not every couple wants. If one of those is your reason, the list below is sorted with that in mind.

1. YNAB: best for a budgeting method

Best for: couples who want to decide together where each dollar goes before it is spent.

YNAB costs $14.99 a month or $109 a year. Its site offers 34 days free with no card when you sign up there; the App Store listing mentions a 30-day trial. Up to six people can share one subscription, each with their own login. One detail worth knowing: YNAB's terms say all budgets are always shared with the group manager. Direct import covers select US, Canadian, UK and EU banks.

YNAB is the pick if Monarch felt like watching money rather than planning it. The head-to-head is in Monarch vs YNAB, and YNAB alternatives covers the rest.

2. EveryDollar: best for starting free

Best for: couples who want a simple zero-based budget and are happy to type transactions in at first.

EveryDollar, from Ramsey Solutions, has a free version you can use for as long as you like, on a computer, phone or tablet. Premium, after a 14-day trial for new users, is $79.99 a year or $17.99 a month and adds bank connect, which pulls transactions in for you. Its page says the household feature lets couples manage one budget together with separate emails. It does not say whether that feature is free or Premium, so check in the app.

3. Rocket Money: best free tier with a partner option

Best for: couples who mostly want to see spending and subscriptions in one place.

Rocket Money has a free plan, a Premium plan where you choose what to pay on a sliding scale (its own guide says typically $7 to $14 a month), and Premium+ at a fixed $15 a month. New Premium members get a 7-day trial. Sharing with a partner through a second login is a Premium feature, as is web access. It runs on iPhone and Android.

4. Goodbudget: best envelope budget

Best for: couples who like the envelope method and want a free plan.

Goodbudget works on the web, Android and iPhone, and its App Store listing says it is made for sharing a budget with a spouse. The Free Forever plan has 10 regular envelopes, 10 more envelopes, 1 account, 2 devices and 1 year of history. Premium is $10 a month or $80 a year on its site ($9.99 and $79.99 in the App Store) and adds 5 devices and automatic bank sync, for US banks only.

5. Quicken Simplifi: best for cash-flow planning

Best for: couples where one person runs the money and the other wants to see the plan.

Simplifi runs on the web and on iOS and Android, connects to US and Canadian accounts, and lets you share with one other person through a sharing space. Its support page says only annual plans are sold and there is no free trial, but there is a 30-day money-back guarantee on purchases from Quicken. When we checked, its site showed a first-year promotion ending 29 November 2026, so see their site for the current price. Its US App Store listing is now called "Quicken: Budget & Track Money".

6. Copilot Money: best for Apple households, with a caveat

Best for: one partner on Apple devices who wants spending, investments and net worth together.

Copilot runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac and the web; Android is not listed. It is $109 a year on both its site and the App Store (its site shows $119 struck through). The monthly price differs: $9 on its site and $18 in the App Store, where there is a one-month free trial. Its US bank connections are listed as US only. The caveat for couples: its site lists inviting your partner as coming this fall, so on 3 November 2026 partner sharing was not yet available. Check before you rely on it.

7. Honeydue: best free couples money app

Best for: couples who want Monarch's shared view without a subscription.

Honeydue is free on iPhone and Android, with optional tips. It connects to banks, lets each partner choose what to share, reminds you about bills and lets you comment on transactions. It is lighter than Monarch on budgeting and planning. See Honeydue alternatives for more.

8. Actual Budget: best open-source option

Best for: couples who want control of their data and do not mind some setup.

Actual Budget is free, MIT-licensed software with desktop downloads and a self-hosted server you open in a browser. It offers multi-device sync and optional end-to-end encryption. US bank sync goes through SimpleFIN, a separate service at $1.50 a month or $15 a year. Separate logins for each partner need an OpenID provider set up. There is no official App Store app.

Where EvenUS fits

Best for: couples whose money question is "is this fair?" rather than "where did it go?".

EvenUS is our app and does a different job from Monarch. You pick one of four ways to split shared costs (by income, evenly, ending up with the same, or splitting what is spare), set simple monthly budgets for the categories you care about, and import a CSV, OFX or QFX statement you download from your bank. Then it counts the hours each of you has left after paid work, travel, the house and the children, and suggests one change a week.

What it will not do matters if you are leaving Monarch: EvenUS never connects to a bank, does not track investments or net worth, and only works for two people. It is in closed testing and not on the app stores yet. One subscription covers both of you: a 7-day free trial with no card, then $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year (USD reference prices, set per region by the app stores). Many couples would run it alongside a budgeting app rather than instead of one. You can try the arithmetic in the fair split calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free alternative to Monarch?

Yes. EveryDollar has a free version, Goodbudget has a Free Forever plan, Rocket Money has a free plan, Honeydue is free, and Actual Budget is free open-source software you host yourself.

Which Monarch alternative gives each partner their own login?

YNAB gives up to six people their own login on one subscription. EveryDollar's household feature uses separate emails. Rocket Money adds a second login on Premium. Simplifi shares a space with one other person. Copilot has announced partner access but it was not live on 3 November 2026.

Is YNAB cheaper than Monarch?

Monthly, they cost the same: $14.99. Yearly, Monarch is $99.99 and YNAB is $109, as listed on 3 November 2026.

Can EvenUS replace Monarch?

No. EvenUS does not connect to banks or track net worth. It covers the fairness side: how shared costs are split and how time and chores are shared.
