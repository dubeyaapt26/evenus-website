Splitwise and Honeydue both help couples with shared money, but they start from opposite ends. Splitwise is a ledger of who paid for what, used by partners, housemates and trip groups, free up to 4 expenses a day with a paid Splitwise Pro whose price is shown in the app rather than on its website. Honeydue is a free, ad-supported app made for couples that links both partners' bank accounts so you can see balances, bills and spending together (both checked 29 October 2026).

  • Choose Splitwise if you want to record and even up shared costs without connecting a bank, split them in many different ways, use it on the web too, or share costs with people beyond your partner.
  • Choose Honeydue if you are a couple in the US who want to link your bank accounts, see both sides of your money in one place, get bill reminders and set monthly category limits, all for free.
  • Choose EvenUS if you are two partners who earn different amounts and want shared costs split by income, alongside a look at free time and who carries the household planning.

What Splitwise does well

Splitwise comes from Splitwise, Inc. in Providence, Rhode Island. It keeps a running tally of shared expenses and the balance between people, and it speaks to couples on its homepage, promising less stress when sharing expenses with a partner. Its App Store listing names couples sharing relationship costs among its uses, and showed a 4.0 rating from about 29K ratings (checked 29 October 2026).

Flexibility is its strong suit. A cost can be split equally, by exact amounts, by percentage, by shares, with an adjustment, item by item, or as a reimbursement on the web. Simplify Debts trims a web of balances down to fewer payments.

When it is time to even up, Settle Up lets you pay through an integration or record a payment made somewhere else. Splitwise Pay works in the US, PayPal and Venmo are US only, and Pay by Bank covers select European countries (UK payments in pounds and euro payments between Germany and France are the examples given). Splitwise also runs on the web as well as on phones, has an offline mode, handles more than 100 currencies and repeats expenses weekly, fortnightly, monthly or yearly.

Where Splitwise is limited

On the free tier you can add up to 4 expenses each day. Unlimited expenses belong to Splitwise Pro, together with currency conversion, charts, receipt scanning, itemisation, expense search, default split settings, card transaction import (select countries) and an ad-free experience. Pro comes as Individual or Duo plans with a Trip Pass, both annual, or as a monthly subscription, and Splitwise does not publish a price on its website.

There is no split by income, so a couple on different salaries works out the percentage or shares and types them in. It also tracks money only: nothing on its official pages covers chores, time or other unpaid work. And it records only what you enter: its help centre says you cannot yet connect it to your bank account, and its pages do not describe a view of your balances or spending.

What Honeydue does well

Honeydue, from Moneydue, Inc. in New York, calls itself the simplest way for couples to track and manage money, together. Where Splitwise is a ledger, Honeydue is a shared window onto your accounts: link your banks, and both of you can see balances, bills and spending in one place. Its App Store listing says it supports most banks in the US, its Google Play listing says over 5,000 banks, and its site says over 20,000 financial institutions across five countries. (The App Store names the seller as WalletIQ, Inc.; its own site and Google Play say Moneydue, Inc.)

Its most thoughtful feature is choice over privacy. Each partner chooses how much to share with the other, rather than everything appearing by default. Transactions are categorised automatically, you can add your own categories, and you can set monthly household spending limits per category with alerts when you near them. Bill reminders sit alongside.

Honeydue also gives money conversations a home inside the app. There is a chat, you can send a thumbs up or one of a handful of other emojis in response to your partner's spending, and, in the words of its Play listing, ask your partner if that mysterious purchase was theirs. You can divide up expenses and square up balances with each other by hand.

Cost is the other draw. Honeydue has no price and no paid feature tier; both store listings flag advertising, its terms mention sponsored links, and its App Store listing shows an optional "Monthly Tip" in-app purchase from $0.99 to $9.99. On the US App Store it was rated 4.5 from 10K ratings, and its Google Play listing shows more than 100,000 downloads (checked 29 October 2026).

Where Honeydue is limited

Linking your banks is what makes Honeydue useful, so it suits couples comfortable connecting their accounts. It is US-first: its site says five countries without naming them, and its store copy talks about US banks. Its site and store listings offer iPhone and Android apps only, with no web app described.

Splitting is by hand; its pages do not describe an income-based split or suggested contributions. Honeydue's joint banking product, Visa cards issued by Sutton Bank, Member FDIC, is shown as a waitlist. Like Splitwise, it tracks money only, with nothing on chores, time or mental load.

Price: Pro in the app against free with ads

Honeydue costs nothing for every feature it has, carries ads, and offers an optional tip if you want to give something back. Splitwise is free as long as you add no more than 4 expenses a day; past that, and for no ads, you would need Splitwise Pro at a price shown in the app; its Duo plan is the one meant for two (checked 29 October 2026).

Sharing with a partner

Splitwise treats a couple as a small group, or as two friends with a running balance. It holds only the shared expenses you enter, not the rest of either partner's money.

Honeydue is built only for two. Both partners link accounts, and each chooses what the other sees. That gives a much fuller picture of household money, along with chat and comments on spending, but it means more of your finances are in view, by design.

How each one splits costs

Splitwise is the more detailed splitter, with seven methods and default split settings in Pro. Honeydue lets you divvy up an expense and square up, but splitting is one feature among many rather than the centre of the app.

Neither describes asking for your incomes. If you agree to a 60/40 split because one of you earns more, Splitwise's percentage mode holds it, and you update it yourselves when pay changes. Our guide to income-proportional bill splitting walks through the arithmetic, and the fair split calculator gives you the percentages to enter.

Privacy and bank linking

This is the sharpest difference. Splitwise says it "doesn't actually handle any real money". Card linking for Pro transaction import (select countries) and bank linking for Splitwise Pay are optional and depend on where you live. You can use it fully without connecting anything.

Honeydue works by linking your bank accounts, and each partner then chooses how much of that to share.

Moving your data or switching

Splitwise can export a group or friendship as a spreadsheet, on the web or in the app. Honeydue's pages do not describe an export from the app, though its privacy policy says you can request a copy of your personal information. Neither app describes importing from the other, so expect to start fresh in either direction. If you think you might move later, take a copy of what you can early.

Which should you choose?

You split a trip or a group bill

Honeydue is for two partners only. Splitwise handles groups, more than 100 currencies and Simplify Debts. Pick Splitwise.

You want to see all your money together, without paying

Honeydue links both partners' accounts, shows balances and bills in one place, and sets category limits, free with ads. Splitwise records only what you type in. Pick Honeydue, if you are in the US and happy to link your banks.

You would rather not connect a bank

Splitwise needs no bank at all. Honeydue is built around linked accounts. Pick Splitwise.

You live together and earn different amounts

Neither describes a split by income. Splitwise's percentage mode lets you enter a fixed ratio; Honeydue's splits are by hand. Pick Splitwise for a set percentage, or look at an app that splits by income, below.

Common questions

Is Splitwise free in 2026?

Splitwise has a free tier that lets you add up to 4 expenses each day. Unlimited expenses, currency conversion, charts, receipt scanning and an ad-free experience come with Splitwise Pro, which is priced in the app rather than on its website (checked 29 October 2026).

Is Honeydue really free?

Yes, as far as its pages show. Honeydue has no price and no paid feature tier; both store listings flag advertising, and its App Store listing shows an optional "Monthly Tip" in-app purchase from $0.99 to $9.99 (checked 29 October 2026).

Which is cheaper, Splitwise or Honeydue?

Honeydue, since every feature is free. Splitwise is also free if you stay within 4 expenses a day; beyond that you would need Splitwise Pro, priced per plan in the app.

Can I move my data from Splitwise to Honeydue?

Not that either app describes. Splitwise can export a group as a spreadsheet, but Honeydue's pages do not mention importing one, so you would start Honeydue fresh with your linked accounts. EvenUS can import a two-person Splitwise group from that spreadsheet.

Does Honeydue have a web version?

Not that its site or store listings describe. Honeydue offers iPhone and Android apps. Splitwise works on iPhone, Android and the web.

Which is better for unmarried couples?

Both work for any couple; nothing on either app's pages limits them to married couples. Splitwise suits partners who keep their finances separate and just want to track shared costs. Honeydue suits couples ready to put their accounts side by side, with each choosing what to share.

Which is more private?

Splitwise works without connecting any bank, and your partner sees only the shared expenses you record. Honeydue links your bank accounts, though each partner chooses how much the other can see.
