Monarch and YNAB are both paid budgeting apps with no free plan, and both cost $14.99 a month (checked 29 October 2026). Monarch is a read-only money tracker that pulls in your accounts and gives a household one shared budget, at $99.99 a year on its Core plan, which suits couples who want everything in one view. YNAB is a zero-based budget built on deciding what your money is for before you spend it, at $109 a year, which suits couples who want to plan each payday and keep some budgets separate.

What Monarch does well

Monarch, from Monarch Money, Inc., is a read-only personal finance app: it gathers your bank, credit card, loan, investment and property accounts into one place for tracking, budgeting, goals and reports. It connects through three data providers, Plaid, MX and Finicity, and you can switch between them. Where a bank will not share a detail, such as a balance, you add it by hand.

For couples, the strongest point is that collaboration costs nothing extra. Monarch's couples page says it costs nothing to add other members to your household under the same subscription, and its help centre says you can invite one or more household members, everyone sits under one subscription, and each person has their own login, as an Admin, Member or Professional.

Budgeting comes in two styles, Flex Budgeting and Category Budgeting, with rollovers that carry a leftover amount into the next month. Around that sit goals, net worth and investment tracking, a bills and subscriptions calendar with reminders, and reports including a Sankey diagram. It is ad-free. On the App Store it shows 4.9 from 116K ratings, and Google Play lists 500K+ downloads (both checked 29 October 2026).

Where Monarch is limited

There is no free plan: the 7-day trial ends with billing unless you cancel at least 24 hours before it ends. Monarch works in the US and Canada only, holds USD and CAD accounts without converting between them, and bills only in US dollars. A household gets one shared dashboard and one shared budget, with no per-person budgets. Shared Views let you mark each account and transaction as shared or as one person's, but Monarch's help centre is direct that you cannot hide bank accounts or transactions from other members. Nothing on its official pages describes splitting shared costs by income or suggesting what each partner contributes, and it covers money only, not chores, time or planning.

What YNAB does well

YNAB (You Need A Budget) dates from 2004 and is built on one idea: decide what your money is for before you spend it. Its current method is framed as "What's it for?", with five steps, For Now, For Later, For Ease, For You and For change. It speaks to people living paycheck to paycheck, paying down debt, on irregular income or arguing with a partner about money.

Sharing is generous. YNAB Together lets up to six people use one subscription, each with their own login, and budgets are not shared automatically: every member can keep their own budgets or share them with the group.

YNAB also works fully without linking a bank. Direct Import pulls transactions in through MX and Plaid if you want it, but manual entry and file import (CSV, OFX, QFX or QIF, on web and iPad) are first-class options. Extras include Targets, net worth reports, Age of Money (the days between earning and spending), a loan planner and widgets. The trial is 34 days with no card on ynab.com, and students can use it free for 365 days with proof of enrolment. On the US App Store it shows 4.8 from 62K ratings, and Google Play lists 1M+ downloads (both checked 29 October 2026).

Where YNAB is limited

There is no free tier after the trial, and the price is in US dollars only, plus tax. In YNAB Together, every budget is always visible to the Group Manager, who also handles billing and can invite or remove people. Like Monarch, it offers no income-based split or suggested contributions, and nothing about chores, time or mental load.

Price: what each costs a couple

Month to month, they match at $14.99. On a yearly plan, Monarch Core is $99.99 and YNAB is $109, so Monarch Core comes out $9.01 a year lower. Monarch's Plus tier is billed yearly; its pricing page does not print the figure, but the US App Store listing shows a Yearly Plus Subscription at $199.99. Neither charges a couple twice: Monarch invites members free under one subscription, and YNAB Together covers up to six people. The trials differ more: Monarch's lasts 7 days and bills you when it ends unless you cancel, while YNAB's lasts 34 days with no card on its website, enough for a month of real paydays. All prices were checked on 29 October 2026.

Sharing with a partner: one budget or several

This is the clearest difference between the two. Monarch gives a household one shared budget and one dashboard. You can label what belongs to whom, but nothing can be hidden, so it fits couples who already pool most of their money.

YNAB treats sharing as a choice per budget. One of you might keep a personal budget while you share a household one, or you might share everything. The catch is that the Group Manager can always see every budget in the group, so "separate" means separate from each other's day-to-day view, not private from the person who manages the subscription. Our guide to budgeting as a couple covers the different set-ups.

How each handles splitting costs

Neither app splits shared costs between partners in proportion to income, and neither suggests what each of you puts in. In Monarch, "split" means dividing one transaction across categories. In YNAB, who pays which share of a shared bill is settled outside the app. Take a couple with take-home pay of 3,000 and 2,000 a month and 2,000 of shared costs: an even split leaves 2,000 and 1,000, while a split by income leaves 1,800 and 1,200. Our fair split calculator runs your own figures, and how to split bills when incomes differ walks through the options.

Privacy and bank linking

Monarch is built around linking: accounts update themselves, read-only, through Plaid, MX or Finicity, with manual accounts for anything else. YNAB makes linking optional, and can run entirely by hand or from bank statement files. Between partners, the difference is the one above: everything visible to the household in Monarch, budgets kept apart from each other but not from the Group Manager in YNAB.

Moving your data or switching

Both let your history out as spreadsheet files, from the web app only. Monarch's help centre describes downloading transaction and balance history as a CSV file. YNAB exports your plan and your transactions as two CSV (or TSV) files, without targets or category notes. Coming in is clearer on YNAB's side: it accepts CSV, OFX, QFX and QIF files on web and iPad, so a file of transactions from elsewhere can be brought across.

Which should you choose?

You pool almost everything and live in the US or Canada

One shared budget, every account linked, nothing hidden: that is Monarch's design. Pick Monarch.

You keep some money separate, or you live outside the US and Canada

YNAB Together lets each of you keep your own budgets and share only the ones you choose, and YNAB works anywhere by hand. Pick YNAB.

You earn different amounts and argue about who pays what

Common questions

Is Monarch or YNAB free in 2026?

No. Neither has a free plan. Monarch offers a 7-day free trial and bills you when it ends unless you cancel; YNAB offers a 34-day free trial with no card required on ynab.com (checked 29 October 2026).

Which is cheaper, Monarch or YNAB?

Monthly, both are $14.99. Yearly, Monarch Core is $99.99 and YNAB is $109, so Monarch Core is $9.01 cheaper. Both cover a partner at no extra charge.

Can my partner and I have separate budgets?

In YNAB, yes: YNAB Together members can keep their own budgets or share them, though the Group Manager can always see every budget. In Monarch, no: a household has one shared budget, and accounts and transactions cannot be hidden from other members.

Does Monarch or YNAB work in the UK?

Monarch is available in the US and Canada only. YNAB imports from UK banks, among others, and can be used anywhere with manual or CSV entry. Both bill in US dollars.

Does Monarch or YNAB split bills by income?

Neither does. Nothing on either app's official pages describes splitting shared costs in proportion to income; in Monarch, "split" means dividing a transaction across categories.

Which is more private, Monarch or YNAB?

YNAB can run without any bank link, using manual entry or statement files, and lets members keep separate budgets from each other. Monarch is built around read-only linked accounts and shows everything in the household to every member.

Do Monarch and YNAB work on Android?

Yes. Monarch runs on web, iOS and Android, and YNAB on web, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Android.
